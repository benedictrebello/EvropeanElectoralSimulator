Methodology

How threshold redistribution works

Seven steps, from ideological position to the seats a failed list ultimately loses.

1

Every list has an economic and a social position

The current 8 parties each sit at a specific (economic, social) point on the compass above. Anything without an explicit position falls back to its single left-right spectrum value on both axes.

2

Distance is measured on whichever axis matters more to that list

Plain straight-line distance treats economics and social values equally. Instead, each list's own economic and social "extremity" (how far it sits from the political center) is compared, and the more extreme axis gets weighted 3× more heavily for that list specifically. A centrist list keeps both axes weighted equally.

3

Distance decays into a weight

Each potential recipient gets a weight of e^(−steepness × distance). The steepness slider in Configuration controls how sharply this falls off — higher values concentrate votes almost entirely on the nearest ideological match; lower values spread them more evenly.

4

Known real-world alliances get a bonus

A handful of documented relationships (Evropa Harmony's ties to the Carlist Party, UCP, and ENLM) get their weight doubled on top of the ideological math — enough to tip a close contest, not enough to override a genuine ideological gap.

5

Separatist and centralist lists don't trade votes easily

If a separatist list's votes would otherwise flow to a centralist list (or vice versa), most of that share — 75% by default — is treated as wasted rather than transferred. The remaining 25% still flows to whichever compatible list is next-best, since not every voter in that position would abstain.

6

Some votes are wasted outright

Before any of the above, the Vote wastage slider in Configuration holds back a share of every failed list's votes as abstention — voters who'd rather not transfer to anyone than compromise.

7

The weakest list goes first, one at a time

Rather than every sub-threshold list redistributing at once, the single weakest one is eliminated and fully redistributed first. Then the list is checked again — if anyone (even a list that just received votes) is still below threshold, the new weakest is eliminated next, and so on until everyone left clears the bar.
